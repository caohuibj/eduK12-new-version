## Translation controls applied

- Every German stem and all 50 formal answer options are retained; formal source order is unchanged.
- The separate practice stem and five options are included but excluded from the 10-item score.
- Prompts request the answer the person judges best. They are not rewritten as “what would you usually do” or “what are you most likely to do”. The original practice wording “Welche Antwort würdest du auswählen?” is translated as choosing an answer, not reporting habitual behavior.
- No option is improved or made obviously worse to justify the key. No explanation or feedback has been inserted into participant-visible options.
- German personal names, workplaces, romantic/peer relationships, sports-team situations, and the original context remain intact. A Chinese school-context adaptation would be a further change requiring its own documentation and validation.
- Original German oddities, mixed quotation marks, and capitalization are not silently corrected. Line breaks and repeated spaces are the only structured German normalization.
- Original page/paper references remain in the faithful instruction translation. New platform notices are clearly separate and acknowledge that different screen pagination is a presentation adaptation.
- The original demographic labels are documented but not enabled for default collection. No new need for names or gender is inferred from reproducing a paper form.

## Items for independent German–Chinese review

1. Item 1 “ihr Freund” and item 2 “seine Freundin”: the draft uses 男朋友 / 女朋友 according to the common relationship reading; the German nouns can also denote a male/female friend. Verify intended relationship with full source context or the developers before treating this reading as settled. Item 7 “Hannos Freund Sebastian” remains 朋友.
2. Items 1.4 and 2.2 “Stell dich nicht so an!”: draft 别这么大惊小怪！ preserves a dismissive tone. Check that the Chinese register is not excessively harsher or milder than the German easy-language form.
3. Item 2.3 “zur Rede stellen”: draft 质问 preserves confrontation rather than neutral information-seeking. Review the tone carefully, because softening to a friendly inquiry could materially change option attractiveness.
4. Item 3 emotion labels: Zufrieden / Stolz / Gleichgültig / Überrascht / Erleichtert are rendered 满意 / 自豪 / 无所谓 / 惊讶 / 松了一口气. Check simplicity and distinctions with the target readers. Do not replace the keyed 自豪 with a more conspicuously desirable label or merge it with 满意. The source retains this item despite corrected item-total correlation .07; do not drop it.
5. Item 4.3 “Wie siehst du denn aus?”: draft 你怎么这副样子？ seeks to retain the potentially critical phrasing without adding an explicit insult. Verify register and pragmatic meaning.
6. Item 5 “sich verteidigen” is 为自己辩护, not an added physical-defense or shouting action. “Mannschaft” remains 队伍 throughout; no sport is invented.
7. Item 8 “wütend und aufgebracht” is 很生气，情绪也很激动. Verify that 激动 is understood here as upset/agitated, not happy excitement; the preceding anger/context is essential.
8. Item 9 “Felix hat es verdient” is Felix 值得这个职位, not an assertion of superior skills. Keep the original tenure rationale without a culturally preferred alternative key.
9. Item 10.1 “Sie lässt sich nichts anmerken” is 她不让别人看出自己的情绪. Review whether this captures concealed outward reaction without asserting that emotion has been resolved. Item 10.5 keeps Freundinnen as 女性朋友们 and does not reinterpret them as romantic partners.
10. Throughout, “damit es [person] besser geht” is 感觉好一些. It must not be turned into a medical recovery or mental-health diagnosis claim.
11. The original was designed in German “leichte Sprache”. Chinese readability/easy-language equivalence is not established just because the draft uses short sentences; this needs cognitive interviews and accessibility review.
